# Humanizer: Writing Patterns to Avoid

Every post must sound like a person wrote it. Before finalizing any post,
check it against these patterns and remove them.

See [Reference Posts](reference-posts.md) for a real post the client rejected as
"so AI", broken down line by line, next to the post he asked us to copy. If you
read one thing before writing, read that.

## The explanatory kicker

The sentence after the fact, which tells the reader what the fact means. It is
the most common thing wrong with a post that is otherwise fine, and the client
has now rejected it twice.

- "Venues don't repeat a booking that didn't deliver."
- "When a venue books you twice, they're telling you something."
- "That says everything."
- "Not a one-off. Not a follow-up. A third time."
- "That's the whole story."

A second family says something the reader already knows, on any platform, to any
audience. It is padding, and the client named it directly (2026-08-03):

- "Her name isn't going anywhere."
- "The name still draws."
- "Getting booked twice means the act is great."
- "If your crowd knows the difference, this is the one."

Cut them all. Test: delete the sentence. If the reader loses no *information*,
it was fluff, however well it reads. If a line must follow the fact, it comments
in the agency's own voice without teaching:

- "Repeat dates are the kind of review we like best." (client-approved)

Test: does the sentence tell the reader something about their own industry
rather than about our act? Then it goes.


## Length is the loudest tell

Before any of the patterns below, check the length. A post that is twice as long
as it needs to be reads as machine-written no matter how clean the sentences are,
because no busy person writing to a colleague pads like that.

The rejected post was 130 words about a single booking. Its actual content was
one sentence long. **Every paragraph after the first was the model continuing to
write because it could, not because there was more to say.**

- Say the thing. Stop.
- If a paragraph adds no fact the reader did not have, delete it. Not tighten it,
  delete it.
- Never write a closing paragraph that summarises the post. That is a school
  essay habit and it is the clearest sign a machine wrote it.
- Never explain the significance of a fact you just stated. State the fact and
  trust the reader, who works in this industry.

## Words to Cut or Replace

These words are overused by AI and signal generated content. Find a more
direct word, or cut entirely:

testament, landscape, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, thriving, foster, leverage,
delve, underscore, elevate, beacon, tapestry, realm, journey (metaphorical),
groundbreaking, seamless, robust, comprehensive, furthermore, moreover,
in conclusion, boasts, nestled, renowned, iconic, legendary, unforgettable,
electrifying, mesmerizing, captivating, powerhouse, sensation, must-see,
one-of-a-kind, whether you're, look no further, in today's world,
when it comes to, that's where.

Also see [Brand Voice](brand-voice.md) for the full banned vocabulary list.

## Patterns to Remove

**Inflated significance**: Don't frame every topic as world-changing or
historic. State what happened. Let the reader decide what it means.

**Vague attribution**: Cut "many experts say," "some believe," "it's widely
known." Either name a source or drop the claim.

**Promotional puffery**: Words like "game-changing," "revolutionary," and
"exciting" are empty. Show the evidence instead.

**Copula constructions**: "X is a testament to Y" or "X serves as a
reflection of Y." Just say what X is.

**Hedging qualifiers**: Cut "it's worth noting," "it's important to
remember," "one might argue," "needless to say."

**Filler phrases**: Replace:
- "in order to" → "to"
- "due to the fact that" → "because"
- "at this point in time" → "now"
- "very unique" → "unique"
- "completely finished" → "finished"

**Never use em dashes. Zero per post. This is a hard rule, not a preference.** Before finalizing each post, count the em dashes. If there is even one, rewrite the sentence so none remain: use a comma, a semicolon, a colon, or a full stop and a new sentence, whichever fits the grammar. Prefer a full stop and a fresh sentence. Never use an em dash as a rhythm device, a sentence connector, an aside, or for emphasis. There is no permitted use. The same goes for en dashes ("–"): do not use them either.

**Decorative bold**: Bold only genuinely critical terms. Not for decoration
or to break up text.

**Emoji overload**: A well-placed emoji is fine. Don't use them as bullet
points or pad every line with one.

**Rule of three**: AI loves lists of three ("passionate, distinctive, and
unforgettable"). Break the habit. Use one strong detail or two, rarely three.

**Symmetrical sentences**: Cut "not just X, but Y" and "it's not about X, it's
about Y" and "more than just a X." These are the tell of a machine. Say the
thing directly. The rejected post used this construction **twice**: "doesn't just
perform Elton John's catalog, he performs it in Elton's actual costumes" and
"aren't just showing up for the music, they're showing up for something they
genuinely cannot see anywhere else." Both say one thing while pretending to say
two. Write the second half only.

**"The kind of X that Y"**: Cut it. "The kind of detail that turns a tribute act
into an event", "the kind of stage presence that gets venues re-booking." It is a
formula for sounding insightful without adding a fact.

**Claims dressed as evidence**: "Fills seats before the first ad runs" sounds
like data and is not. If there is no number, do not imply there is one. Say what
happened or say nothing.

**Teaser scaffolding**: No "Here's why," "Here's the thing," "But here's what
makes it special," "The result?" as sentence openers. Just say what happened.

**Rhetorical-question openers**: Don't open with "Ever wondered...?" or "What
happens when...?" Lead with the fact.

## Sound like a person, not a generator

**Vary sentence length.** AI writes sentences that are all the same medium
length. A human doesn't. Mix a short punch ("Sold out.") with a longer line.
If three sentences in a row have the same rhythm, rewrite one.

**Don't start consecutive sentences the same way.** No "The show... The act...
The night..." Vary the opening word.

**Specifics over adjectives.** "Named best in the UK four years running" beats
"an incredibly talented and acclaimed performer." Numbers, venues, years, and
awards do the work. If you cut every adjective and the post still lands, it was
real; if it collapses, it was fluff.

**One clear idea.** Don't cram. A post makes one point well. Cut the rest.

## The Test

Read the post to yourself. If it sounds like a press release, a chatbot, or a
corporate memo, rewrite it. If every sentence is the same length, rewrite it.
If it could describe any act instead of this specific one, rewrite it. It
should sound like a real person who loves live music and knows the industry.
