# Reference Posts: What Failed, What to Copy

Two real posts, both supplied by the client. Read this file before writing
anything for LinkedIn. Rules tell you what to avoid; these show you the
difference between a post that got rejected and one the client wants to imitate.

## The post the client rejected

Sent to the client on 2026-07-31. Their reply: "I'm so sorry that I just think
this is so AI."

> The Rocket Man Show is booked for August 1st at Garden Theatre in Winter
> Garden, Florida.
>
> Rus Anderson doesn't just perform Elton John's catalog, he performs it in
> Elton's actual costumes from the 1970s. Personally owned. Period-accurate. The
> kind of detail that turns a tribute act into an event.
>
> This is the type of booking that fills seats before the first ad runs.
> Audiences aren't just showing up for the music, they're showing up for
> something they genuinely cannot see anywhere else.
>
> The Rocket Man Show has built its reputation on exactly this: production
> value, authenticity, and the kind of stage presence that gets venues
> re-booking before the night is over.
>
> If you have a date to fill and want an act that delivers without question,
> reach out at info@loveproductions.com to check availability.

### Why it failed

Every one of these is a rule elsewhere in this graph. It broke all of them at
once, which is what "so AI" actually means.

1. **Far too long.** Roughly 130 words and five paragraphs. The client's words:
   "Are not quite sure writing several paragraphs about the show if someone is in
   the business. I can't see how that's helping sell this."
2. **Two symmetrical "not just X, Y" constructions.** "doesn't just perform his
   catalog, he performs it in..." and "aren't just showing up for the music,
   they're showing up for...". This is the single loudest AI tell in the post.
3. **A rule of three in the closing paragraph.** "production value,
   authenticity, and the kind of stage presence that...".
4. **It buries the only real fact.** Rus Anderson owns and performs in Elton
   John's actual 1970s costumes. That is genuinely rare, checkable, and the whole
   reason to book this act. It appears in paragraph two and is then smothered by
   three paragraphs of agency-speak.
5. **Paragraphs 3 and 4 contain no information at all.** Strip the adjectives and
   nothing remains. "Fills seats before the first ad runs" is a claim with no
   number behind it.
6. **It is a show announcement wearing a credibility post's clothes.** It opens
   with a date and a venue, which belongs on Instagram and Facebook, not here.

### What it should have been

> Rus Anderson performs Elton John's catalogue in Elton's actual costumes from
> the 1970s. The boots, the glasses, the jumpsuits. He owns them.
>
> Booking: [calendar link]

Around 200 characters. One fact, the one nobody else can claim, stated plainly.
No adjectives doing the work of evidence. Nothing to wade through.

## The post the client wants us to imitate

Steve sent this Instagram post from **Reliant Talent Agency**, another booking
agency, as the model to follow (instagram.com/p/DbMQ4wrknkD, 2026-07-31).

**The entire caption:**

> Our clients are booked and busy! Swipe to see some of the tours that are on
> sale now.

Then three hashtags. That is all of it: 17 words, two sentences.

**The format:** a 7-slide carousel. Each slide is one act's tour admat, the full
date list rendered as a poster, with SOLD OUT flagged in red beside individual
dates.

### What to take from it

- **The graphic carries the message; the caption only points at it.** The dates
  and the sold-out flags are the proof. The copy does not describe them,
  interpret them, or sell them. Do not write a paragraph explaining a picture.
- **Length.** 17 words. When a post has a strong visual, the caption should get
  shorter, not longer.
- **Plain language.** "Booked and busy" is how a person talks. There is no
  "production value", no "delivers without question", no "the kind of X that Y".
- **Proof over adjectives.** Nobody claims the acts are in demand. They show a
  wall of dates, several sold out, and let the reader conclude it.
- **No pitch.** No availability line, no "if you have a date to fill". The post
  earns attention first.

### What NOT to take from it

Reliant has roughly 2,900 followers and this post earned 68 likes. Copy the
format because the client asked for it and because it is genuinely tighter than
what we were producing. Do not treat it as proof of a channel that is winning.

## The test, before any post goes out

1. Read it aloud. Does it sound like the rejected post or the Reliant one?
2. Cut every adjective. Is anything left? If not, there was no post.
3. Count the sentences. On LinkedIn, more than four means cut.
4. Find the one fact a competitor could not also claim. Is it in the first line?
   If it is in paragraph two, move it up. If there isn't one, do not post.
