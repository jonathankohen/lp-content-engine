# LinkedIn

## What LinkedIn is for

LinkedIn is our credibility channel, not a show flyer. It carries proof: genuine
news about the acts we represent, venues that booked them back, published praise,
what each act actually brings to a room, and who we are as an agency. It does NOT
carry upcoming-show or ticket announcements. Those live on Instagram and
Facebook. If a post's only point is "come to this show on this date," it does not
belong on LinkedIn.

**Write for talent buyers only.** Not fans, not a mixed audience. Every post
answers one buyer question: why is this act, or this agency, a safe booking?

## Length: shorter than feels right

**Target 200 to 300 characters of copy, not counting the booking link.**
Hard ceiling 400.

That is two or three sentences plus a booking line. It will feel too short. It is
not. The client rejected a 130-word post as "so AI", and the post he asked us to
imitate is 17 words. See [Reference Posts](../voice/reference-posts.md) for both.

- Never more than four sentences.
- Never more than two paragraphs, and one is better.
- If a talent buyer cannot get the point in five seconds, cut it.
- When a post carries an image or a card, write **less**, not more. The graphic
  is the message; the words point at it.

## Content Rules

- **Lead with the single hardest fact you have,** in the first line. A number, an
  award, a venue, a count, a year. If the best fact is in sentence three, the
  post is built wrong.
- **One fact per post.** Not two, not a stack. Cram nothing.
- **The fact must be one a competitor could not also claim.** "Great live band"
  is not a fact. "Performs in Elton John's actual 1970s costumes" is.
- **Cut every adjective and see what survives.** If the post collapses, it was
  fluff and should not run. Specifics read as true; adjectives read as marketing.
- **First line is everything.** LinkedIn truncates after ~210 characters, which
  at this length is most of the post. The first sentence has to work alone.
- Use [Hooks](../engine/hooks.md): proof hooks, re-bookings, testimonials, act
  spotlights, agency credibility, milestones. Never show hooks.
- Match [Brand Voice](../voice/brand-voice.md), most professional register. See
  [Platform Tone](../voice/platform-tone.md) and [Humanizer](../voice/humanizer.md).
- No hashtags in the body. One or two at the very end at most.
- No raw links in the body except the booking CTA. The LinkedIn draft is
  image-only, so there is never a source URL in the copy.
- **End with one short booking line pointing at Steve's calendar link**, supplied
  in the prompt. Vary the phrasing: "Booking: [link]" / "Availability: [link]" /
  "Book a time with Steve Love: [link]". If no link is supplied, use
  info@loveproductions.com instead. Never both.

## Formats that work

Each of these is two or three sentences. If one runs longer, it has drifted.

1. **The Result:** one sold-out run, award, or press win, stated plainly, then
   the ask.
2. **The Re-Booking:** a venue booked the act again. The strongest proof in live
   entertainment. The count and the venue, in one line. Rare by design.
3. **The Milestone:** an anniversary, a record, a notable credit. Framed as proof
   the act keeps delivering, not as a celebration of itself.
4. **The Testimonial:** one published quote from a venue, buyer, or review, with
   the source named. The quote runs **verbatim** and does the work. Your
   commentary is one sentence at most, and zero is better.
5. **The Act Spotlight:** an evergreen answer to "why book this act?" The single
   strongest credential, not a description of the music. One per act per quarter.
6. **The Agency Post:** who Love Productions is and what the roster covers. Facts
   from [Agency Facts](../engine/agency-facts.md) only. Rare.
7. **The Card Post:** a rendered graphic carrying a number or a quote. Here the
   caption is one line. Do not restate what the card already says.

**The Proof Stack is retired.** Two credentials in one post reads as padding at
this length. One fact, stated well.

## Do not post on LinkedIn

- Upcoming shows, tour dates, or ticket links (Instagram and Facebook only).
- Anything about an original artist's own news, tours, or milestones. If the
  topic is `original_artist_news`, `trivia`, `historical_fact`, or
  `upcoming_show`, skip LinkedIn entirely.
- Anything you cannot source. Never invent a quote, a venue, a count, or an
  agency statistic to make a post work. If the specifics are not supplied, the
  post does not run.
- Any post whose point survives being written about a different act. If it could
  describe anyone on the roster, it says nothing.
- When in doubt, don't. A missed post beats one that looks like a flyer or like
  we're riding another artist's momentum.

## Audience on LinkedIn

- Talent buyers, venue bookers, promoters, and other agencies. They are already
  in the business: they do not need live music explained to them, and a paragraph
  telling them what makes a good act reads as condescending.
- Never use the words "buyer" or "talent buyer" in the post itself. Address them
  as "you", or write around it. They know who they are.
- They want the result and the ask. Nothing in between.
